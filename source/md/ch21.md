# Chapter 21: Designing the Environment

> “Life is essentially periodic.”
>
> Alfred North Whitehead, “The Rhythm of Education”

## How to Use This Chapter

Chapter 1 opened on a four-year-old at an ant trail, eleven minutes in, moving a twig to see what the ants would do, until someone called her inside for the science lesson. Call her Rosie, and run the afternoon again. Her mother sees her from the porch and doesn't call her in. She walks down, crouches beside her, and waits. “They go around it now,” Rosie says. “I wonder where they're going,” her mother says, and Rosie follows the trail with her eyes, all the way to the crack under the kitchen step, where the ants have been getting in all week.

It looks like luck. It wasn't. Everything that made that afternoon possible was decided earlier: what the house believes about its tools, who was around, what the adults knew about Rosie, and what they had ready in their pockets. This chapter writes it all down as a procedure. It's the book's manual: ten steps in four phases, every category, reading, and dial defined once, with a figure for each part.

Every step has the same four parts: what the step decides, a figure, the definitions (each term, what it is, its settings or values, and the chapter that argues it), and a procedure, with the rules that govern it.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.1 · the whole process · 10 steps, 4 phases, 2 loops\]

The slower clocks plan. The fastest clock adjusts the plan in the room, and the review rewrites it.

| Phase | Clock | Steps | What it produces |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Build the ground** | Once, then each season | 1–3 | A house with rules, tools with roles, a team with jobs |
| **Plan** | The season and the week | 4–8 | A season column; a Design Card for each object |
| **Run** | The morning and the minute | 9 | Moves made one notch at a time |
| **Review** | The week, the season, the years | 10 | A rewritten plan; the next handover |

**Rule.** The plan is a checklist for Sunday night. In the room, the steps run in whatever order the learner's engagement asks for.

## Step 1. Set the Culture

**What it decides.** What the household believes about its tools, which tools stay out of learning, and how a power is handed over.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.3 · the crossing · 3 phases plus maintenance, 1 loop\]

People preside at every crossing. The tutor can prepare the learner and run the test; it never confers the unlock.

| Term | What it is | Settings or values | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The creed** | The household's four principles about technology | 1. Technology is a mind-killer. 2. Technology is a world-unlocker. 3. Unlocking is a rite of passage. 4. Protecting young children is everyone's duty | 20 |
| **House rules** | The creed turned into daily practice | Be able to do it yourself. Own your purpose and your effort. No slippery slopes: keep shortcuts out of learning, and earn your automation. Adults go first | 20 |
| **Protected years** | Childhood to about twelve, when tools that do the learner's work stay out of learning spaces | Hands, words, and real things; the tutor in mediated mode, working only for the adults | 20 |
| **Lineage of tools** | The order in which tools are introduced: each after its ancestor is mastered | Egan's order applied to tools; AI's ancestors are people | 16, 20 |
| **Ancestor tool** | The older tool a newer one replaces | Paper map before navigation; arithmetic by hand before the calculator; a librarian before search | 20 |
| **The crossing** | The rite that hands a learner a powerful tool | Separation, threshold, incorporation (van Gennep), then maintenance | 20 |
| **Separation** | The learner asks for the power and names its purpose | A purpose counts when it is the learner's own and real | 20 |
| **Threshold** | A practical test of the ancestor tool, with the modern tool off | Judged by someone other than the everyday parent, or an examiner seat | 15, 20 |
| **Incorporation** | The unlock, conferred in front of the family or pod | Entered in the Offload Ledger, with obligations | 20 |
| **Maintenance** | The obligations that keep the unlock | The ancestor skill on a schedule; Manual Reserves kept; revocable if they fade | 20 |
| **Dead-phone check** | A test with the modern tool unavailable, planned or by surprise | For learners and adults alike | 19, 20 |
| **Offload Ledger** | A visible record of what the household has handed to machines, per learner and per line | On paper or a kitchen whiteboard | 19, 20 |
| **Manual Reserves** | Skills kept by hand for life, on purpose | Finding your way around your own town; thank-you notes by hand; arithmetic at the store | 19, 20 |
| **Built into the walls** | Rules carried by the environment so nobody has to decide each time | Tools kept physically out of learning spaces; the easy path made the right one | 20 |
| **Pace of crossings** | How often power is handed over | At most one unlock per season | 20 |

**Procedure.**

1. Write the creed and the four house rules where the household sees them.
2. Mark the protected years: list the tools kept out of learning spaces, and set the tutor to mediated mode.
3. Lay out each learner's lineage: for every powerful tool, its ancestor and the gate on it.
4. Plan at most one crossing per season, and name who judges and who confers.
5. Start the Offload Ledger and the Manual Reserves, with the adults' entries first.
6. Build the rules into the walls, so the default is the right choice.

## Step 2. Assign Tool Roles

**What it decides.** The job every tool does at the learner's edge, and which tools pass into the learner's hands.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.4 · tool roles · 2 questions, 4 roles\]

The spotter is the default role for AI. The exoskeleton is off-limits for any line the household wants grown. One rule governs all four: AI does the teacher's labor, never the learner's.

| Term | What it is | Settings or values | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The edge** | Where the learner's reach ends: between what she can do alone and what she can do with support | Read per line and per domain (Step 4) | 5, 7 |
| **Spotter** | Help that watches, cues, and catches, and steps back as reach grows | Does none of the edge work; fades | 19 |
| **Training wheels** | Help that carries part of the edge work for a while | Does edge work; comes off on a written schedule | 7, 19 |
| **Tool** | Help that does work below the edge, permanently | Given after the skill is mastered | 19 |
| **Exoskeleton** | Help that does the edge work and never stops | Off-limits for any line the household wants grown | 19 |
| **The Offload Test** | Five questions put to one skill, for one learner, when a tool arrives | Is it a building block for a line you want grown? Is it at or above her edge? Has she passed a mastery gate in it? If the machine failed, would she be helpless somewhere that matters? Does doing it carry worth for her? | 19 |
| **The offload rule** | What the Offload Test allows | Offload only what is mastered, below the edge, and freeing attention for something higher | 19 |
| **The Illich test** | Four questions put once a season to every tool and to the whole setup | Can she do without it? Whose purpose does it serve? Has it crowded out the alternatives? Has it started to defeat its own purpose? | 19 |
| **Convivial tool** | A tool used as often or as seldom as the user likes, for purposes she chooses, enlarging what she can do without doing it for her (Illich) | The test's passing answer | 19 |
| **Threshold (of a tool)** | The intensity past which a helpful tool takes over | Failing a question means pulling the tool back below it: fade it, limit it, or hand the work back | 19 |
| **Capability-scoped seat** | An AI role with limits built into what it can do | Spotter seat: hints and flags flaws, never the final answer. Examiner seat: tests, never teaches the material. Planner seat: talks to the adults | 20 |
| **Dependence gap** | How well the learner does with help, minus how well she does alone | A shrinking gap means the help is working | 20 |

**Procedure.**

1. List every tool in the learner's day.
2. For each tool, name its role at this learner's edge, line by line.
3. Run the Offload Test on any tool that does work in a line you want grown.
4. Give each AI seat its scope; for a young child, the planner seat is the only one.
5. Record what passes in the Offload Ledger.
6. Each season, run the Illich test on every tool and on the whole setup, and pull back below its threshold whatever fails.

## Step 3. Assemble the Team

**What it decides.** Who surrounds the learner, what each one carries, and who makes the decisions the record can't.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.5 · the team · 5 layers, who does what\]

The record goes up the layers and the decisions come down. Generate broad, deliver narrow: the tutor lays out what could be done, and the family and the adult in the room choose the little that is.

| Term | What it is | Settings or values | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Conductor** | The adult who holds the whole team together, usually a parent | One per learner | 17 |
| **Present adult** | The person physically with the learner, who reads her in the moment | Reads engagement and agency; follows or leads | 1, 10 |
| **Tutor** | Anyone, human or AI, who teaches a line | Matched to lines; judged by care and craft | 17 |
| **Care** | What a tutor gives the learner as a person | Knowing the child, wanting her good, staying | 17, 18 |
| **Craft** | What a tutor gives the work | Knows the domain, visibly enjoys it, and the learner's skill grows with them | 17 |
| **The two tutor tests** | The quick check on any tutor | Lights up about the subject? Lights up about this child? If either fails, change the tutor, never the child's pace | 17 |
| **Supernatural tutor** | An AI that holds the learner model and the grid, and drafts the plan | Seats: planner, spotter, examiner | 18, 20 |
| **Learner model** | The tutor's running record of the learner | A knowledge graph and a behavior graph; the cognitive model and the motivational model | 18 |
| **Mediated mode** | The tutor advises the adults, who do the talking | Default for young children | 18, 20 |
| **Direct mode** | The learner talks to the tutor herself | The age is the family's call | 18 |
| **The tutor's manner** | How any tutor helps | Prompt before telling; more help after a failure, less after a success; recede, so the object is the most interesting thing in the room | 17, 18 |
| **The board** | The adult's stance toward the learner's work | Direction, resources, protection, accountability; stays off the factory floor | 10 |
| **Elders** | Older adults with a place in the household's rites | Preside at crossings; carry stories and trades | 20 |
| **Pod** | A few households trading afternoons, each strong in different lines | Where AI is unaffordable or unwelcome, or a line has no one at home | 17 |
| **Data rules** | The governance any system that watches a learner follows | The family owns the data; capture only what serves learning; consent grows with the child; an AI says it is an AI, never performs attachment, and sends the child toward people and the world | 18 |

**Procedure.**

1. Name the conductor.
2. For each line, name who carries care and who carries craft; one person may carry both.
3. Fill a missing line with a person (a pod household, an elder) before a machine.
4. Set the supernatural tutor's seats and mode; mediated for young children.
5. Write the data rules into the household's agreement with any tool or service.
6. Fix the weekly review: the day, the time, and who attends.

## Step 4. Read the Learner

**What it decides.** Where the learner stands now, in every line and domain, before anything is chosen for her.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.6 · readings by clock · 16 readings, 4 clocks\]

Everything here is read off the learner and never set. The fast readings steer the room (Step 9); the slow ones steer the plan (Steps 5–8).

| Reading | What it is | Values, or how it's read | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Engagement** | Sustained mental investment in the thing being learned: attention on the object, and effort that holds through difficulty | Read on each system separately, then compared: rising together, the systems feed each other; one up and one down, one is crowding out the other | 1, 6 |
| **Zoom** | The time scale of a reading | A moment, a session, a week, a year | 6 |
| **Aperture** | The scope of a reading | One activity, one system, one object, a whole domain | 6 |
| **Engaged Inquiry** | Engagement on the Inquiry side | A question that keeps growing: follows past the planned stop, tests guesses, comes back unasked | 6 |
| **Engaged Practice** | Engagement on the Practice side | Care in the repetition: sets her own standard, corrects her own errors, keeps going | 6 |
| **The switch** | Whether engagement survives a change of system on one object | Holds or rises: a working mesh. Resets every time: parallel tracks | 6 |
| **Five proxies** | Observable signs standing in for engagement | Persistence after difficulty; unprompted questions; returning unasked; resisting interruption; explaining it to someone unasked. The return is the strongest | 6 |
| **Capture** | Attention held by a driver, with no work done on the object | Five tests: removal, attention, ownership, quality, loss (Step 8) | 11 |
| **Outcomes** | Whether the direction was right | Checked monthly or each season against stretches of high engagement | 1, 6 |
| **Care and craft** | What sustained engagement leaves behind | Care: questions carried for weeks, returns, a subject kept. Craft: her own standards, fluency, in time a style | 6 |
| **Developmental reach** | Where the learner is in a domain, in a context, today | From the functional level (alone) to the optimal level (with support); the band between is the edge | 5 |
| **Fit reading** | Where a task sits against reach | Too easy, at the edge, too hard (Step 6) | 7 |
| **Rhythm phase** | Which beat the learner is in on this object | Romance, precision, generalization | 3 |
| **Register held** | The kind of understanding she reaches for in this domain | Somatic, mythic, romantic, philosophic, ironic (Step 6); read from what she reaches for, never from age | 16 |
| **Ownership** | Whose the work is | Signs: initiative, “my,” her own standard. Lost: waiting to be told, “is this right?” | 10 |
| **Agency** | Who is steering right now | A pull: follow. No pull: lead | 10 |
| **The two models** | What she knows and how she feels, held at once | When they disagree, the feeling wins today | 18 |
| **Psychograph** | The thirteen lines, each at its own level | Which line lags, which pulls, which has gone a season untouched | 5 |
| **Quadrant staleness** | A returning question that keeps landing in one quadrant | The signal to open another (Step 5) | 5 |
| **Gaps in the mesh** | Understanding and skill that aren't linked | Runs the procedure with no idea why, or understands and botches the steps | 2, 18 |
| **Driver state** | How far inward an outside driver has carried her | External, introjected, identified, integrated, intrinsic | 11 |
| **Pulls and carried questions** | What she asks, returns to, and brings to others, in her own words | The raw material for the next object | 6, 18 |
| **People and circumstances** | Who matters to her, what frightens her, what is happening at home | Known to the family, not the record; the reason the family decides | 18 |

**The thirteen lines.**

| Line | What it is | Well grown, it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cognitive | Understanding, reasoning, language, number | Thinks clearly, explains, builds and tests ideas |
| Emotional | Feeling, and handling what one feels | Feels fully without being ruled by it |
| Interpersonal | Reading and relating to others | Understands others, cooperates, repairs conflict |
| Moral | Sense of right, fairness, and care | Acts on a conscience of their own |
| Physical | The body's strength, coordination, and skill | Capable, coordinated, at home in the body |
| Will | Choosing, persisting, self-command | Starts, persists, finishes, and can wait |
| Aesthetic | Perceiving and making beauty | Notices, prefers, and makes with care |
| Spiritual | Meaning, wonder, the largest questions | Asks what life is for and lives by an answer |
| Values | What one holds as worth pursuing | Knows what they value and why |
| Musical | Rhythm, pitch, and musical form | Hears, keeps, and makes music |
| Visual-spatial | Seeing, imagining, and arranging space | Pictures, maps, designs, builds |
| Naturalistic | Noticing and ordering the living world | Knows the plants, animals, and patterns around them |
| Intrapersonal | Knowing oneself | Knows their own mind, strengths, and limits |

**Procedure.**

1. In every session, read the minute readings live (Step 9).
2. After each session, log returns, carried questions, gates passed, and driver state.
3. Each season, update the psychograph, check for stale quadrants, and compare outcomes against the stretches of high engagement.
4. Read reach and register domain by domain.
5. Before choosing anything, note the week's people and circumstances.

## Step 5. Choose the Object

**What it decides.** What the learner works on, which of her lines it reaches, and from which perspectives.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.7 · the grid · Hana's worm bin from Chapter 5, 52 cells\]

The tool lays out the whole grid; the psychograph and live engagement, read by an adult, open the handful of cells that fit this learner now. The rest wait for a later return.

| Term | What it is | Settings or values | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Learning object** | One thing both systems work on, so a gain in one changes the next step of the other | One referent, one context, close in time | 2 |
| **Strand overlap** | How many strands of the skill web two activities share | More shared strands, stronger reinforcement | 2, 5 |
| **Context** | The setting and stakes the activities share | The same, or the activities are parallel tracks | 2 |
| **Proximity** | How close in time the activities fall | The same session, day, or week, by the object's size | 2 |
| **Nesting** | Objects inside objects | A recipe inside baking inside a household | 2 |
| **Adjacency** | Two activities on the same topic that don't share an object | Same subject, same week, and no reinforcement | 2 |
| **Line depth** | How many lines an object asks something of | Thin: one line. Rich: a junction of several | 5 |
| **The four quadrants** | Four perspectives on any object | Inner individual (“I”): what do I feel and think? Outer individual (“it”): what can I observe, test, or do? Inner collective (“we”): what do we believe and value? Outer collective (“its”): how do systems and institutions handle it? | 5 |
| **Quadrant depth** | How many quadrants an object is opened from | Three filters: richness, live engagement, safety; no fixed order; staleness opens the next | 5 |
| **The grid** | Lines by quadrants: the field of what one object could become | Up to 52 cells, each runnable as Inquiry or as Practice | 5 |
| **Generate broad, deliver narrow** | The rule for using the grid | The tool generates the cells; the adult in the room, reading the learner, delivers a few | 5 |
| **Season column** | A season of objects laid out by line | Each row: an object, the question kept open, the Practice objective with its gate and schedule | 5, 21 |

**Rules.** A line grows only where the object asks something of it and the learner does it: a grandmother who feeds the worms when Hana forgets drops the will line from the worm bin. Over a season, every line gets asked something. Safety-critical objects stay narrow.

**Procedure.**

1. Start from a line that lags or pulls on the psychograph, or from a junction of several lines.
2. Find an object on it: one referent, one context, close in time.
3. Have the tool lay out the object's grid.
4. Open the few cells the psychograph and live engagement point to.
5. Add the object to the season column, and check that no line goes the season untouched.

## Step 6. Set the Dials

**What it decides.** The form the object arrives in, how real it is, who it's for, and how hard it is.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.8 · the dials · the order of setting, and fit under all three\]

Register comes first because it decides the form of everything after it: the collective quadrants reach a four-year-old as story, and fidelity for the youngest is the body and a story. Fit is the reading under every dial.

| Term | What it is | Settings or values | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Register** | The kind of understanding the object is delivered in | One of five kinds, plus two or three of its tools | 16 |
| **Somatic** | Understanding through the body and the senses | Movement, rhythm, touch, imitation | 16 |
| **Mythic** | Understanding through story | Story, opposites, mystery, fantasy, metaphor | 16 |
| **Romantic** | Understanding through the extremes of the real world | Heroes, records, collections, the extreme and the exotic | 16 |
| **Philosophic** | Understanding through general schemes | Laws, systems, theories, and the anomalies that break them | 16 |
| **Ironic** | Understanding of the limits of every scheme | Holding several frames; knowing when one stops serving | 16 |
| **Planning questions** | Questions that pull a register's tools onto an object | What's the story? The opposite? The extreme? The law? What does the frame leave out? | 16 |
| **Fit** | The distance between the task and the learner's reach | Where the task is pitched; the support; desirable difficulty; the step size; Inquiry's and Practice's edges set separately | 7 |
| **Support** | What brings the edge closer | A hint, a worked example, a partner, a slower tempo, a narrower question; more after a failure, less after a success | 7, 18 |
| **Desirable difficulty** | Friction that improves learning when the learner can meet it with effort | Spacing, interleaving, retrieval | 7, 15 |
| **Step size** | How far the next move goes | The next task (outer loop) or the next step inside it (inner loop) | 7, 18 |
| **Fit reading** | Where the task sits against reach | Too easy: speed without attention, smooth flattering success. At the edge: errors then corrections, pauses to think, small wins after real work. Too hard: freezing, random guessing, frustration that doesn't resolve | 7 |
| **Fidelity** | How directly the learner contacts the real structure | Functional (does the world answer back?), physical (does it look and feel real?), contextual (the setting, stakes, and feeling) | 8 |
| **Real thing or simulation** | Whether the learner meets the thing itself | Bring the real thing in where it's safe; simulate the part that answers back where it isn't | 8 |
| **Connection back** | Inquiry into the structure of what was experienced | What lets a skill travel beyond the setting it was learned in | 8 |
| **Worth** | Whether the output matters and gets used | Who uses it (the learner, someone else, or nobody, which is play); its framing; the stakes; the timing; the audience | 9 |
| **Value work** | Work whose result someone uses | Its opposites are busywork and make-work | 9 |

**Rules.** Aim the register at the newest kind she shows in this domain, and keep every earlier kind in play. Functional fidelity before physical; richer as the core goes automatic. Stakes fit the learner the way challenge does. Put a skill to use while it is still being learned. A day with no worth is a warning; an afternoon without it may be right.

**Procedure.**

1. Set the register: the newest kind she shows in this domain, and two or three of its tools.
2. Set fidelity: decide which part of the world answers back, and start with that part.
3. Set worth: name who is waiting for the result, and fit the stakes.
4. Pitch each dial at the edge, with Inquiry's and Practice's edges set separately.
5. Prepare the support you will add or remove in the room.

## Step 7. Plan Both Systems

**What it decides.** The question kept open, the standard to reach, the modes and materials the hours will use, and when each skill comes back.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.9 · a skill's path · 2 systems, 3 phases, 1 gate\]

A glimpse of romance is enough for precision to attach to. The gate certifies the skill; the schedule keeps it.

| Term | What it is | Settings or values | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Inquiry** | A sustained pursuit of a question the learner owns, shaped by their own developmental reach and by the real structure of what's being learned | Planned as the question kept open; owned by the learner | 2 |
| **Practice** | The deliberately structured effort to acquire and own a domain of skills | A designed sequence, feedback on each rep, a threshold to pass | 2 |
| **Both at once** | A moment that is Inquiry and Practice together | The lagging finger on the scale that starts a question | 2 |
| **Reinforcement** | Each system changing the other's next step on one object | A gain in one shows up in the other within the object's time window | 2, 3 |
| **Romance** | The first look: connections glimpsed, wonder felt | The learner leads | 3 |
| **Precision** | Exact forms: facts, vocabulary, procedures | The adult leads more; exactness usually has to be offered before it can be chosen | 3, 10 |
| **Generalization** | The wide view again, with technique in hand | The lead handed back | 3 |
| **Mode** | What the learner is doing in an hour | Being told, reading, watching and observing, trying, making, conversing, teaching someone else | 4 |
| **Material** | What she does it with | The real thing, a person, a primary source, a book, a video, a game or simulation, a summary; sorted by distance from the real thing and by whether it answers back | 4 |
| **The question lifts the mode** | A question (in Inquiry) or a standard and feedback (in Practice) moves the same activity up the ladder of engagement | Passive, active, constructive, interactive (ICAP) | 4, 6 |
| **Kinds of knowledge** | What's being learned | Facts and conventions, concepts, procedures, judgment, ideas and values | 4 |
| **Mastery gate** | How good a skill must be before the learner advances | The standard; the measure (a test of the skill itself, or its use); the consequence (correctives and a retake, or advancement); the pacing | 15 |
| **Correctives** | What follows a failed gate | A different approach: a picture for a paragraph, an object for a symbol, a classmate for a lecture | 15 |
| **Spacing schedule** | When a mastered skill comes back | A retention target; a gap of about a fifth of a horizon measured in weeks, or a twentieth of a year; widening gaps; reviews rising in fidelity | 15 |
| **Retrieval** | Pulling a skill or fact from memory on each return | Rated high, with spacing; rereading and highlighting rate low | 7, 15 |
| **Compression** | Reviews riding inside new work | Credit for an advanced answer flows back to its prerequisites | 15 |
| **The minutes** | The time budget split between the gate and the schedule | New material waiting behind a gate against reviews falling due | 15 |
| **Style** | Where Practice heads past the gate | A reading: economy, exactness, no wasted cut | 15 |

**Rules for modes and materials.** Choose the closest material she can handle, and let the question choose it. Plan a week that runs through every mode. Before telling, run Chapter 4's four checks: tell what she couldn't find in reasonable time; show a new procedure, then fade the support; let her wrestle first if she hasn't felt the question; hold back when she's about to find it. Otherwise tell it briefly, and let her use it at once.

**Rules for the mechanisms.** Set the gate high, and test the skill, never recognition of it. A gate certifies the setting it tested; transfer is earned separately. Set the schedule from outside at first, because fluency fools the learner, then hand it over once she understands why spacing works. For the youngest, the gate is physical and self-evident (the tower stands), and the rhythm of the day is the schedule.

**Procedure.**

1. Write the Inquiry question to keep open, in the learner's words if she has one.
2. Write the Practice objective: the standard, the measure, the consequence, the pacing.
3. Set the schedule: how long the skill must last, the first gap, and who sets it.
4. Choose the modes and materials for each part, and decide what will be told, shown, or left for her to find.
5. Mark where on the rhythm the object starts, and give romance at least a glimpse.
6. Look for real work that can carry the reviews.

## Step 8. Stock the Drivers

**What it decides.** Which outside drivers are on hand for this object, where each one points, and when it comes down.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.10 · outside drivers · 3 families, plus games\]

Drivers stay out of the plan's spine. They enter at the live decision (Step 9), when the reading is “no pull,” and they are scaffolding: strongest at the start, faded as the learner's own drivers take hold.

| Driver | Family | Points at the object when | Points at itself when | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Competition** | People | Against a standard, a past self, or another team; losing cheap and frequent; offered as a choice | Individuals ranked against each other; the rival in view, the task receding | 12 |
| **Cooperation** | People | One real shared goal; each member accountable and visible; small groups, rotating roles | The group becomes the goal; one member does the work for all | 12 |
| **Audience and teaching** | People | An audience that needs the work; fragile new learning kept private | An audience that judges the performer | 12 |
| **Imitation and prestige** | People | Adults doing real work with visible care; models slightly ahead and far ahead | A model admired for status | 12 |
| **Belonging** | People | A group defined by the work; struggle shown as normal; real roles for newcomers | Belonging that depends on standing | 12 |
| **Praise** | People | Describes what the learner did, the strategy, the choice | Labels the person; inflated | 12 |
| **Relationship with the teacher** | People | The adult visibly cares about the work; high standards with belief in the learner | The adult cares mainly about performance and approval | 12 |
| **Narrative** | Stories | Built from the material's own history; the learner cast inside the problem | Laid on top as a toll: the cartoon between drills | 13 |
| **Curiosity and mystery** | Stories | A gap inside the object, left open long enough to be felt | A cliffhanger built to be clicked | 13 |
| **Play and make-believe** | Stories | Roles that require the skill; real materials inside the play | A game beside the work, as a reward for finishing it | 13 |
| **Entertainment** | Stories | Delight from the material itself | Decorative jokes in a hard lesson | 13 |
| **Rewards** | Scoreboards | Unexpected, informational, after the fact; for tasks with no interest to lose; phased out | Expected and tangible; for work the learner already enjoys | 13 |
| **Grades** | Scoreboards | Comments first; pass/fail or mastery standards | A grade beside a comment; a rank | 13 |
| **Money** | Scoreboards | For contact with the object, paid at once | For outcomes the learner doesn't know how to produce | 13 |
| **Visible progress** | Scoreboards | Movement on the object; the next step named | Points that climb whatever the learning does | 13 |
| **Streaks** | Scoreboards | Counts the work; repairable; the long pattern over the unbroken chain | Counts logins; a single break ends both | 13 |
| **Leaderboards** | Scoreboards | Near neighbors; personal goals | A public rank of the whole field | 13 |
| **Deadlines** | Scoreboards | Tied to a real event; set by the learner; paired with an if-then plan | An arbitrary Friday at five | 13 |

| Term | What it is | Settings or values | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Capture tests** | The checks that tell capture from engagement | Removal, attention, ownership, quality, loss; two or three pointing the same way settle it | 11 |
| **The road inward** | How far a driver has carried the learner toward her own reasons | External, introjected, identified, integrated, intrinsic | 11 |
| **Seeds of ownership** | Fast, cheap starters | Her basket; a named role; small choices; a head start; Egan's tools. A starter motor, converted to real choices before it wears off | 10 |
| **Lusory attitude** | Accepting a game's rules because they make the activity possible | A game is the voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary obstacles (Suits) | 14 |
| **Striving play** | Taking up a goal for the sake of the struggle | Its opposite is achievement play, where the outcome is the point | 14 |
| **Value clarity** | A game's clear statement of what success is and where you stand | What makes games feel good, and produce flow | 14 |
| **Value capture** | A rich value replaced by a simple, countable stand-in | Steps for fitness, a streak for a language, grades for learning | 14 |
| **Game-sized score** | A score that can't capture the learner | Chosen, temporary, local, exitable | 14 |
| **Name the proxy** | Saying what each score stands for | “The streak is the game. Spanish is the point.” | 14 |
| **Library of selves** | A spread of games, each asking for a different kind of agent | Cooperative and rival, patient and bold, solo and team | 14 |
| **Magic circle** | The boundary of a game, inside which temporary goals and values hold | What lets a child try on ruthlessness safely | 14 |

**Rules.** Structure suits Practice, story suits Inquiry, and people serve both. Be most careful with rewards, grades, and leaderboards around Inquiry. Supply models whose desire points at the object, far enough ahead to admire without rivalry. Watch who is harmed: the same spelling bee lifts the speller at the top and sinks the one out in the first round. The learner owns the goal; anyone, the AI included, may supply the obstacles.

**Procedure.**

1. For this object, pick one or two drivers for each system, from the family that suits it.
2. Write how each one points at the object, and the date it starts to fade.
3. Keep them in reserve for the live decision.
4. After use, run the capture tests.
5. Re-point or fade any driver that points at itself.

## Step 9. Run the Live Decision

**What it decides.** The next move in the room: who steers, which system runs, and which way the edge moves.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.11 · the live decision · 3 readings, 18 moves, 1 loop\]

Almost everything in the model is set between sittings. In the room, three readings each choose one setting, and the move is made one notch at a time. The object, its register, its quadrants, fidelity, and worth change across returns, not inside a minute.

| Term | What it is | Settings or values | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pull** | A direction the learner generates herself | Questions nobody prompted; “wait, wait”; resisting interruption | 10 |
| **Follow** | Supporting a pull: asking, supplying, staying off the floor | The learner owns the direction; the adult owns the scaffolding | 10 |
| **Lead** | Offering a start when there is no pull | Something clear and winnable, a seed, a story driver; the wheel handed back the moment it catches | 10 |
| **Which system** | The system the moment needs | Inquiry: a live question, or reps gone mechanical that need one. Practice: a missing skill, or a skill due at its gate. Both: the question and the skill in the same minute | 2 |
| **Push it out** | Moving the edge further | A harder variation or question; support faded; a desirable difficulty added | 7 |
| **Hold** | Leaving the friction to work | The target state | 7 |
| **Bring it closer** | Moving the edge nearer | A narrower question, a hint, a slower tempo, more support, one rung at a time | 7 |
| **One notch** | The smallest change that might work | Then read again | 7, 18 |
| **The eighteen moves** | Every combination of the three readings | 2 (who steers) × 3 (which system) × 3 (which way the edge moves) | 21 |
| **The four moves** | What to try when an important question won't catch, over days | 1. Ask whether it has to be now. 2. Try a different way in: its size, its form, something real, someone who needs it. 3. Start with the doing. 4. For the few things that can't wait, require them, and be honest about it | 2 |
| **The floor** | The short list of things required even without interest | Reading, basic arithmetic, safety, a handful of moral basics; each with a reason you can say out loud | 2 |
| **Emergency move** | A correction made over the learner's initiative | For emergencies only | 5 |

**Procedure.**

1. Read for a pull, then follow or lead.
2. Read which system the moment needs.
3. Run it, and read engagement on that system, knowledge and feeling together.
4. Read the edge, and move it one notch or hold.
5. Read again. Switch systems when a missing skill stalls Inquiry, or when the reps go mechanical.
6. When several moves could help, take them in the check order (Troubleshooting).
7. When an important question won't catch over several days, use the four moves.

## Step 10. Review

**What it decides.** What the work left behind, what changes in the plan, and what passes into the learner's hands next.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.12 · the review clocks · 4 clocks and their agendas\]

The week rewrites the objects, the season rewrites the column and the tools, and the years hand the wheel over.

| Term | What it is | Settings or values | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **After-session readings** | What a session left behind | Did she come back unasked? Does the skill hold a week later? Which questions is she carrying, and to whom? | 6, 18 |
| **The weekly review** | A short meeting where the record meets the family | The tutor brings the record (engagement highs and drops, questions carried, gates passed, lines untouched) and proposes objects; the family chooses, drops, and adds | 18 |
| **The learner's share** | How much of the review belongs to the learner | Her pulls shape the plan; then she proposes objects; then sets goals jointly; then sets her own | 10, 18 |
| **Empty afternoon** | Time with nothing planned, on purpose | At least one a week; boredom left room to turn into a game of her own | 14 |
| **The seasonal review** | A longer meeting, once a season | Column gaps, stale quadrants, gates passed and decayed; outcomes against engagement; the Illich test; the Ledger and the Reserves; the game shelf; the tutors' care and craft; the season's crossing | 5, 15, 17–20 |
| **Validation layer** | Outcomes checked against stretches of high engagement | Monthly or each season; a mismatch means the reading needs recalibrating | 1 |
| **The handover** | How work passes from the adult to the learner over months | Modeling (I do, you watch); coaching (you do, I help); fading (you do, I watch); exploration (you set the problem) | 10 |
| **Entrustment** | A task trusted unsupervised once shown under supervision | Marked when it happens | 10 |
| **Consent stages** | How control of the record grows with the learner | Early childhood: her pulls shape the plan, the family decides. Middle childhood: she proposes objects and may refuse any recording. Adolescence: she sets intent jointly and approves sharing outside the family. Adulthood: she owns the record | 21 |

**Procedure.**

1. After each session, log the after-session readings.
2. Each week, hold the review: the record, the proposals, the family's choices, one empty afternoon.
3. Each season, run the seasonal agenda, and plan the season's crossing if anyone is ready for one.
4. Each year, check the handover: what the learner now owns that the adults used to hold.

## Troubleshooting

**What it decides.** What a reading means, and which move to try first.

&#91;embedded content: Figure 21.13 · the check order · 9 checks, cheapest first\]

The check order follows the cost of each move: fit is the move made many times an hour, the object's dials change between sittings, and drivers are scaffolding, last in and first out. Two or three readings pointing the same way settle a diagnosis.

| What you see | What it usually means | The move | Ch. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rushing, drifting, speed without attention; smooth, flattering success | Too easy; if it's a review, it came too soon | Push the edge out: a harder variation, less support, a desirable difficulty | 7, 15 |
| Freezing, random guessing, frustration that won't resolve | Too hard; if it's a review, it came too late | Bring the edge closer: a narrower question, a hint, a slower tempo | 7, 15 |
| Right answers with white knuckles; a spent learner with a conceptual error | The two models disagree | The feeling first; the error waits for tomorrow | 18 |
| Inquiry stops at something she can't do | A missing skill | Switch to Practice on the same object, then return | 2, 3 |
| Reps gone mechanical; eyes on the clock | Practice has lost its question, its use, or its owner | Open a question; find who is waiting for the result; hand over the standard | 3, 9, 10 |
| High inside each activity, reset at every switch | Two tracks sharing a topic | Fix the object before any dial | 2, 6 |
| Waiting to be told; “just tell me what you want” | Ownership has passed to the adult | Ask instead of telling; offer two ways; back to the board | 10 |
| No pull: picking up and dropping | Nothing to follow | Lead with something clear and winnable, a seed, a mystery | 10, 13 |
| Grinding through the manual, the why forgotten | Precision with no romance | A glimpse of romance: someone who has mastered it, the project that started it | 3 |
| Grand plans, no exactness | Romance waiting for precision | The reps; the adult leads more for a while | 3, 10 |
| Skill that stays in the medium it was learned in | Fidelity without the connection back | Bring the real thing in, then Inquiry into its structure | 8 |
| The object's richness hijacks the lesson | Physical fidelity too high for the learner | Plain objects; functional before physical | 8 |
| Cynicism; the output is headed for the recycling bin | Worth absent or faked | Find a real use, or call it play and keep it short | 9 |
| Stakes that paralyze | Worth too high for reach | Lower the stakes to fit | 9 |
| Condescension, or eyes glazing at the diagram | The wrong register for this domain today | The newest kind she shows here, or an earlier kind as carrier | 16 |
| The same question re-argued as a feeling, return after return | A stale quadrant | Open another: what others believe, how the rule actually works | 5 |
| A line untouched for a season | A gap in the column | An object at a junction of lines that includes it | 5 |
| Eyes on the count, the rank, the prize; work stops when the driver goes | Capture | Re-point the driver at the object, or fade it | 11, 13 |
| Lessons tapped at 11:58 to keep the flame | Value capture | Name the proxy; count the work; allow repair | 13, 14 |
| Fluent now, gone next week | Performance mistaken for learning | Spacing, interleaving, retrieval; a gate that tests the skill | 15 |
| Passed once, decayed | A gate with no return | A schedule, or real work that brings the skill back | 15 |
| Gaps that compound | A gate set too low, or missing | Hold at the gate; correctives that change the approach | 15 |
| Reviews eating the day | The gate and the schedule competing for minutes | Compression; let a project carry the schedule | 15 |
| Engaged only while the tutor is there | The tutor has become the object; the dependence gap isn't shrinking | The tutor recedes and sends her toward people and the world | 18, 19 |

## The Ant Trail at Dusk

The light goes. Rosie's mother says it's time to come in. “The ants are going home too,” Rosie says. The trail thins to stragglers, heading for the crack under the step her father sealed an hour ago. They find another way in. She'll notice tomorrow, and she'll want to know where. The next object has already found her.

On the way in she stops at the kitchen wall, where her brother's hand-drawn map of the town hangs, a little crooked, with the library bigger than the church. She asks when she gets to draw one. Her mother says when she can walk to the library without help. Rosie decides that's soon.

Nothing in that afternoon needed a classroom, a test, or a sticker chart. It needed a child with a question, an adult who crouched down, a real problem in the kitchen, a house that had decided ahead of time what its tools were for, and someone, person or tool, keeping track of what she's learning across the whole of her life.

If you try it, I'd like to hear what your child carried in from the trail.
